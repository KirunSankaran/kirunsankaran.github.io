Philosophy 8: Introduction to Moral Philosophy

Dartmouth College — Autumn 2025 Section 2A: Tuesday/Thursday 2:25–4:15 (2AX Wednesday 5:30–6:20), Silsby Hall 213 Instructor: Kirun Sankaran (Silsby 106) Office hours: M 2–3pm; Th 1–2, or by appointment

Course Description

How should we live? How should we live together? We're going to examine a bunch of questions about how to live well, and figure out how to start answering them.

Learning Goals

Students should leave class with an understanding of various issues surrounding questions of how we ought to live. They should also have skills in reconstructing and formulating philosophical arguments.

Assignments

  1. Nine (9) short essays, written collaboratively and partially in-class, on assigned topics (20%)
  2. One (1) in-class midterm exam (25%)
  3. One (1) final paper (25%)
  4. Class attendance and participation (30%)

Attendance and Participation

You learn philosophy by doing it. This class does not work unless you come to class having done the assigned reading, and actually discuss it, which means your participation is incredibly important. So in general I will expect you to attend every class. However, you're adults and stuff happens. So you get two no-excuse skips for the quarter, beyond which, absent a Dean's note, your participation grade will be knocked down by a third of a letter grade.

Screen Policy

Screens distract you and they distract your classmates. Laptops and cell phones are not permitted in class, except when (1) I have given explicit permission for a specific assignment or in-class activity, or (2) the SAS office has instructed me to allow an electronic device as an accommodation (in which case I'll ask that you sit at the back of the classroom). To fully engage in class discussion and activities, I encourage you to print out the readings and bring them to class, and take handwritten notes.

ChatGPT and Other LLMs

I encourage the use of Large Language Models like OpenAI's ChatGPT or Anthropic's Claude as an aid to your learning. As a rule, if you use it to aid you in your writing, I expect you to be up-front and honest about the division of labor between it and you. More specific guidance will be provided on assignment sheets and in class.
