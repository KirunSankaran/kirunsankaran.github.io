PHIL 273: Justice, Rights, and the Common Good

Philosophical Perspectives on Social and Economic Issues — UNC–Chapel Hill, Fall 2022 Meetings: MWF 12:20–1:10, Peabody 3050 Instructor: Kirun Sankaran (Caldwell Hall 214A) Texts: A.O. Hirschman, Exit, Voice, and Loyalty; Russell Hardin, Indeterminacy and Society (all other texts on Sakai)

Course Description

This course focuses on justice and the common good, applying theoretical justifications to contemporary social and economic issues. Readings include classical and contemporary literature on the nature of justice and rights. Open to philosophy majors/minors, PPE minors, and all interested undergraduates.

Course Format

Class-wide discussion, small-group discussion and collaborative projects, group presentations, and debates, with short lectures for context and clarification. Nearly every meeting involves a significant group-work component assessed in the participation grade. Students also take part in a peer-review exercise.

Assignments and Assessment

  1. Three reading responses — 20% each. Eighteen readings are listed; pick three and write ~1000 words: (a) outline the reading's argument, (b) raise a question for the author, (c) outline a potential response on the author's behalf.
  2. Final paper — 25%. A ~2000-word paper on the philosophical dimensions of a pressing policy problem other than climate change, extending the reasoning of one course reading to that case. Includes a rough draft and in-class peer review (worth 10%, so the final paper itself is 15%).
  3. Attendance and participation — 15%. Three no-penalty absences; each further unexcused absence costs a third of a letter grade. Participation depends largely on the quality of contributions to in-class group work and presentations.
